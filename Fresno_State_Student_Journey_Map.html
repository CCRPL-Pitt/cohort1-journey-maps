⚑ Stage 1 Gap Summary
Preliminary Milestones
Admitted student melt of roughly 7 percent peaks in the post-Dog Days summer gap, with no structured re-engagement until the first day of class. Technology activation (Duo, portal) creates drop-off before enrollment is confirmed, and the portal itself has been described internally as needing a significant update that has not yet happened.
Financial Access
Students do not understand that FAFSA is an annual requirement. Financial aid office hours (8am to 5pm) are inaccessible to commuter and working students. Focus groups described the financial aid office as understaffed and difficult to reach, with one student needing two hours of driving just to complete a payment.
Academic Foundation
Major misalignment begins at the application stage; students are presented with an exhaustive list of majors on Cal State Apply with little context. The change-of-major process after Dog Days is confusing and creates early advising barriers, sometimes setting students up to fail rather than succeed.
Advising & Planning
Dog Days is information-overloaded and students retain very little; advisors report students falling asleep in appointments. Advisor caseloads near 800 to 1 make meaningful one-on-one advising structurally difficult at orientation. Block scheduling creates friction for dual enrollment students.
Belonging & Engagement
Communication overload (125+ emails) drives disengagement. No structured activity exists between Dog Days and the first day of class, and additional welcome events after Dog Days have been described internally as unsuccessful. Welcome Week is not fully designed for a commuter campus.
(Mental) Health & Basic Needs
Not formally mapped in Stage 1: no proactive wellness introduction, mental health screening, or basic needs intake at orientation. The Student Cupboard, Essential Needs, and Parent Scholars programs exist but are not introduced as a Stage 1 milestone.
Career & Future Self
Career guidance is largely absent from recruitment and orientation. Students are asked to select a major from an exhaustive list with little career context, and no formal career exploration milestone exists before the first day of class.

⚑ Stage 2 Gap Summary -- Year One Enrollment
Financial Access
20 to 25 percent of students carry financial holds. The $900 threshold creates registration blocks that are not always paired with proactive outreach. SAP consequences, especially MAX-time issues with no formal warning semester, are not well communicated. FAFSA renewal surprise, students believing it is one-and-done like admissions, is a major cause of financial disruption in Year 1.
Academic Foundation
No campus-wide early alert system exists, and where informal alerts do occur, application is inconsistent across departments. Students who have not completed the Golden 4 face the highest stop-out risk on campus. Dual enrollment credit misalignment creates unexpected GE gaps, particularly in Math.
Advising & Planning
Advisor caseloads near 800 to 1 make proactive outreach structurally difficult. Students describe the DPR as lacking a clear visual progress indicator, with an accurate replacement not expected until 2027. Block scheduling protects Year 1 enrollment but leaves students unpracticed at independent registration heading into Year 2.
Belonging & Engagement
Belonging is described as developing through specific relationships, not programs, yet structural investment in those relationships is insufficient. Commuter students remain most underserved. Students are frequently bounced between offices without a single point of contact.
(Mental) Health & Basic Needs
Shame is repeatedly named as the primary barrier to help-seeking. Financial aid, counseling, and Essential Needs all operate 8am to 5pm only. The Student Cupboard's in-library pop-up partnership is described as an effective, low-barrier model worth expanding.
Career & Future Self
Career planning begins too late and is not embedded in curriculum or classroom conversation. First-generation students have no career mentorship outside the institution if career services does not intentionally reach them in Year 1.

⚑ Stage 3 Gap Summary -- Year Two Enrollment
Financial Access
The same cascading hold and registration system continues. Students who entered Year 1 with unresolved financial confusion now face compounded risk, and disengaged students may simply stop enrolling rather than navigate hold resolution.
Academic Foundation
Major declaration delays peak here. The DPR is confusing and frequently inaccurate, with an update not expected until 2027. Students who did not pass the Golden 4 are at risk of stopping out at this stage. Roadmaps are inconsistent across departments.
Advising & Planning
The sophomore transition is the most predictable and least-addressed drop-off. Block enrollment ends, making independent registration a new and unsupported skill. Fewer than 15 coaches support every student on academic notice or warning campus-wide.
Belonging & Engagement
Cohort belonging from block enrollment disappears. Sophomore students feel abandoned. Commuter students remain most underserved. No universal sophomore-experience program exists.
(Mental) Health & Basic Needs
Mental health, financial, and family pressures peak in Years 2 through 3. Most support efforts remain focused on Year 1. Students on academic warning are the most likely to disengage from all support systems at once.
Career & Future Self
Nearly 60 percent of students hold unpaid internships, an equity barrier for low-income students. Career planning is optional and is not reaching most students at this stage.

⚑ Stage 4 Gap Summary -- Year Three Enrollment
Financial Access
Students in Year 3 who have accumulated balances from Year 1 or 2 face compounded debt, and continued enrollment is increasingly at risk. Small financial barriers in Years 3 and 4 have outsized consequences.
Academic Foundation
Students are unsure if they are on track; DPR confusion persists. Every course section is described as full in every department. Course access is the single greatest barrier to timely graduation for UDT students; 100 percent of UDT students are expected to be delayed by it. Transfer students face significant advising challenges around credit recognition.
Advising & Planning
Inconsistencies exist in advising access and quality across departments. Technology makes running academic audits difficult, limiting advisors' ability to catch problems proactively in Years 3 and 4.
Belonging & Engagement
Support and outreach for Years 3 and 4 students is significantly less robust than for Year 1 students. Transfer students in Year 3 have limited time to build campus-based belonging before graduation.
(Mental) Health & Basic Needs
Students in Year 3 face financial, housing, and mental health barriers, yet most support and outreach still focuses on first- and second-year students. Stop-out risk in Year 3 is often driven by life pressures rather than academic performance alone.
Career & Future Self
Career prep remains optional and late for most students. Nearly 60 percent are engaged in unpaid internships, an equity barrier for low-income students. Career guidance often comes too late for students who have already accumulated the wrong courses.

⚑ Stage 5 Gap Summary -- Year Four to Graduation
Financial Access
Small financial barriers at graduation have disproportionate consequences. Completion grants are not yet available at scale. Surprise costs, fees and regalia, affect low-income students. Loan repayment is not genuinely understood at exit.
Academic Foundation
Near-completers are not systematically tracked. DPR inaccuracies create late-stage discovery of missing requirements, including courses not offered in years. Course availability closures are most painful at this stage. Misadvising consequences are felt most acutely at graduation.
Advising & Planning
Graduation audit authority (course substitutions by deans and chairs) exists but is used inconsistently. Students with catalog-year course gaps face an inequitable burden with no consistent resolution process.
Belonging & Engagement
Identity-affirming celebrations exist, but accessibility for commuter, caregiving, and housing-insecure students is not tracked. Capturing alumni contact information is a challenge after graduation.
(Mental) Health & Basic Needs
No post-graduation transition support is documented for students who relied on campus essential needs resources. The Student Cupboard is not available to alumni, and there is no documented warm referral pathway to community resources at graduation.
Career & Future Self
Students often graduate without competitive experiential credentials. Career guidance is sought too late. Underemployment is a primary concern. Alumni access to the Career Development Center is not communicated proactively.
