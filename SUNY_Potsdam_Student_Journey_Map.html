⚑ Stage 1 Gap Summary
Preliminary Milestones
Information overload without a unified checklist. First-gen and rural students arrive without a navigational framework for institutional systems. The 2-year on-campus housing requirement creates financial conflict for students who planned to live at home. Transfer credit evaluation delays disrupt registration for 2+2 students.
Financial Access
SmartTrack award letters are difficult to interpret without financial literacy scaffolding. Former foster youth face structural FAFSA barriers with no documented institutional workaround. Part-time students face TAP eligibility disadvantages. Billing confusion causes anxiety when students do not understand aid disbursement timing.
Academic Foundation
Technology platform proficiency cannot be assumed. BearDeN and BearPAWS navigation is unintuitive for students unfamiliar with degree audit systems. Transfer credit evaluation timing may leave transfer students registering without a complete picture of how their prior coursework applies.
Advising & Planning
First-gen students arrive without cultural familiarity with how advising works, what questions to ask, and how to advocate for themselves. Transfer students need a distinct advising pathway integrating credit evaluation with degree planning rather than the standard first-year advising script.
Belonging & Engagement
Orientation is primarily designed for traditional residential students. Transfer students, adult learners, and commuters experience a thinner version. Online students may miss orientation entirely. Welcome Weekend information overload leaves first-gen students without clear guidance on what to prioritize.
Health, Wellness & Basic Needs
CCC staffing shortage creates long wait times. Lack of counselor diversity is a documented barrier for students from underrepresented backgrounds. Basic needs resources (food pantry, case manager) are available but not consistently promoted at orientation. Off-campus and online students are largely unaware of available resources.
Career & Future Self
Platform fatigue limits Handshake engagement. The opt-in career services model systematically misses first-gen students who most need early career capital. No curricular integration for career exploration at Stage 1.

⚑ Stage 2 Gap Summary
Financial Access
Students do not always understand SAP requirements until after they lose aid. FAFSA renewal is treated as students' responsibility without sufficient institutional scaffolding. Part-time students face structural aid disadvantages. Outstanding bill balances create recurring anxiety, particularly for students whose aid has not yet posted.
Academic Foundation
Black students complete only 58.3% of attempted Year 1 credits vs. 85.4% for White students -- a 21-point gap that is the most significant documented disparity in the institution's data. Starfish early alert requires consistent faculty participation that is not uniformly delivered. No remedial coursework exists; WAYS courses must be completed or repeated the following semester, creating a compounding credit accumulation risk.
Advising & Planning
Advising quality varies by department. First-gen students are least likely to proactively initiate contact with advisors. Transition from faculty advisors to professional advisors is in progress; students in time-sensitive majors (such as Education) must declare in Year 1 or fall off the 4-year graduation timeline.
Belonging & Engagement
The 2-year on-campus housing requirement shapes all belonging programming around residential experiences; commuters and working students are structurally excluded from many touchpoints. Students of color navigating predominantly white institutional spaces experience belonging as more conditional and effortful. Students from the JCC extension site do not have the same campus experience as main campus students.
Health, Wellness & Basic Needs
CCC wait times remain high and counselor diversity is low. Year 1 stress peaks are not consistently matched with proactive outreach. Off-campus and working students cannot access services during standard business hours. Cumulative racialized stress is invisible to academic performance monitoring systems.
Career & Future Self
Opt-in career services model systematically reaches students who are already engaged. First-gen and low-income students who most need early career capital are least likely to self-initiate. Handshake engagement remains low despite the connection between on-campus employment and student persistence.

⚑ Stage 3 Gap Summary
Financial Access
Off-campus housing transition in Year 3 introduces food insecurity and transportation insecurity risk for low-income students. Institutional investment in affordable on-campus housing options is a key retention lever for students in financial difficulty.
Academic Foundation
Credit accumulation gaps from Year 1 compound in Year 2 if not addressed through targeted support. Students who have not declared a major or who are drifting without a clear academic plan face growing time-to-degree risk. BearDeN credit audit accuracy, especially for transfer students, needs consistent monitoring.
Advising & Planning
Major declaration without career counseling integration is particularly consequential for first-gen students who have had less exposure to professional career pathways. Students with specialty admission requirements (nursing, education) may discover additional application requirements after enrolling in the major. Housing decision counseling is not consistently integrated into advising conversations.
Belonging & Engagement
Off-campus housing transition attenuates belonging for students without intentional reconnection programming. Working students and commuters remain structurally limited in campus life participation. Students of color continue to navigate belonging in predominantly white institutional spaces with insufficient culturally affirming programming.
Career & Future Self
Low-income students face a structural barrier to unpaid internship participation. CSTEP resources are underutilized outside the program's direct membership. Professional organization participation is low even when foundation funding is available.

⚑ Stage 4 Gap Summary
Advising & Planning
The Junior Check is the most important institutional progress review in the journey. Students who miss it or arrive with undetected sequencing issues face a critical intervention moment. The single check-in model cannot compensate for years of limited advising engagement for first-gen students. The follow-up protocol for students who miss the meeting is not yet documented in Starfish.
Financial Access
Part-time students and those on extended timelines are approaching federal aid eligibility limits without proactive counseling. Aid structures built for 4-year trajectories create disadvantages for adult learners and students who have navigated stops and starts in their enrollment.
Belonging & Engagement
Off-campus students experience a gradual loss of passive belonging infrastructure that is difficult to reverse without deliberate outreach. Leadership opportunities with high time commitments remain inaccessible to working students. Imposter syndrome and self-doubt suppress leadership participation among academically capable students, particularly first-gen students and students of color.
Career & Future Self
Low-income students face structural barriers to unpaid internship participation. Professional organization participation is low even when CSTEP and foundation funding exist. CSTEP resources are transformative for eligible students but are not widely known across the broader student body. Only 1 of 10 qualified students applied for a fully funded national honor society conference.

⚑ Stage 5 Gap Summary
Financial Access
Loan exit counseling arrives too late to change borrowing decisions. Students who run out of graduation credits but have also exhausted financial aid eligibility face a compounding crisis with limited institutional options. Financial holds block graduation clearance for students who cannot pay final balances. Income-based repayment counseling at graduation can still reduce harm even if it cannot undo earlier debt accumulation.
Academic Foundation
Students frequently underestimate what is required beyond credit hours. General education, major requirements, and residency requirements can be invisible until the graduation audit. Students on extended timelines navigate more complex requirements. Mental health challenges in the final stretch create academic risk at the most consequential moment. Not all majors or minors display completely in BearDeN.
Advising & Planning
Graduation application holds at 100+ credits are a useful institutional intervention -- but only if they trigger an advising appointment rather than just a hold. Students who received misadvising face the largest consequences at graduation review, with the least margin to absorb an additional semester.
Belonging & Engagement
Cost of regalia and family travel barriers prevent first-gen and low-income students from fully participating in commencement. Grad Finale timing creates access problems for working students with schedule conflicts. Online students arriving for their first in-person campus experience at graduation need an explicit institutional welcome. Regalia donation program is in development but not yet launched.
Career & Future Self
Students who deferred internship or capstone completion due to financial necessity arrive at Stage 5 in crisis mode. FDS response rates likely underrepresent outcomes for high-need populations -- first-gen, Pell-eligible, and Black students -- creating data blind spots precisely where the institution most needs visibility. Post-graduation transition support is not consistently provided for first-gen students who are navigating life after college without family models for these transitions.
