Accept Admission and Pay Enrollment Deposit
Preliminary Milestones & Orientation⚑ GAP✏ Team Input
▾
▸ Student Actions
  • Receive admission decision and next-steps communication
  • Pay $50 non-refundable enrollment deposit (deadline: July 1)
  • Activate university account and set up DUO authentication
  • Submit final high school transcripts and any in-progress college coursework
  • Submit meningitis documentation
  • Complete TSI (Texas Success Initiative) requirements or establish exemption
  • Finalize residency status if applicable
▸ Institutional Actions
  • Send admission decision with clear next-steps checklist (Offices: Admissions, Registrar, Student Health Center, Testing Center, SAM Center)
  • Provide immediate account activation instructions and DUO setup support
  • Send targeted text/call reminders for incomplete checklist items
  • Process transcripts, residency reviews, and meningitis documentation promptly
  • Remove holds quickly when items are received; provide tech support access
⚠ Pain Points & Barriers
  • Post-admission process is lengthy; students must complete multiple separate items before orientation or registration can proceed
  • The $50 non-refundable deposit is a barrier for low-income and foster youth students whose financial aid has not yet been packaged
  • DUO authentication is a documented friction point; many students struggle with setup and receive no timely tech support
  • Admissions and Housing operate as separate communication streams with no unified checklist or timeline
  • July 1 deadline disadvantages students who are still deciding or waiting on financial aid information
◈ Gaps & Disparities
  • First-generation students without tech-savvy family support are more likely to get stuck at account activation, often their first impression of navigating institutional systems
  • Former foster youth and high-need students are unaware of available fee waivers; reducing the number of choices and creating a unified checklist would reduce overwhelm
  • Late registrants (25–30% register May–July) face a structural disadvantage that compounds for first-gen and low-income students who lack the social capital to act earlier
✏ Team Input Needed

The $50 non-refundable deposit and the DUO authentication barrier are documented, but the institutional actions column does not describe a fee waiver process or a proactive identification system for students who need one. Is there a current protocol for identifying former foster youth, high-need, and low-income students at the point of admission and proactively connecting them to deposit waivers before the July 1 deadline? What is the current DUO support pathway for students who get stuck; is it documented, staffed, and accessible outside business hours? And what is the actual late-registration rate disaggregated by first-gen and Pell status; does the data confirm that late registrants are disproportionately the students who most need early advising?

Complete TSI Assessment and Satisfy Requirements
👤 Marisol (Low-Income, First-Gen) · Marcus (First-Gen, Male)
Preliminary Milestones & Orientation⚑ GAP✏ Team Input
▾
▸ Student Actions
  • Determine TSI status: exempt (via SAT/ACT scores, prior degrees, GED, or dual credit) or required
  • If required, complete TSI assessment prior to registration
  • If below threshold, enroll in required developmental education (Dev Ed) coursework
▸ Institutional Actions
  • Testing Center processes TSI assessments daily and determines student placement
  • Communicate TSI requirements and all exemption criteria clearly at point of admission
  • Offer two Dev Ed options: no-credit/no-cost and no-credit/with-cost
  • Provide co-requisite math and English pathways to preserve credit-hour momentum
⚠ Pain Points & Barriers
  • TSI is a documented barrier; students who fail to meet requirements are routed into Dev Ed, which delays progress and adds cost without earning degree-applicable credit
  • First-gen students may be unaware of their exemption options, leading to unnecessary testing or missed exemptions
  • High school GPA is a better predictor of college readiness than the TSI score, yet placement decisions rely heavily on test outcomes
◈ Gaps & Disparities
  • Low-income and first-gen students are disproportionately routed into Dev Ed, adding semesters, cost, and departure risk without earning credit toward graduation
  • Students placed in Dev Ed for English face the highest dropout risk of any gateway scenario; a proactive, targeted intervention at this milestone is among the highest-leverage investments SHSU can make for student persistence
✏ Team Input Needed

What percentage of incoming students require TSI testing vs. qualify for exemptions? Of students routed to Dev Ed, what is the Fall-to-Fall persistence rate vs. students who test out? Are co-requisite English and Math options currently available, and at what scale? The session noted gateway English failure is a dropout predictor; how is this risk being identified and addressed proactively before the first day of class?

Register for Orientation (Required) and Attend
Preliminary Milestones & Orientation⚑ GAP✏ Team Input
▾
▸ Student Actions
  • Register for an Orientation session (first session May 29; weekly sessions continue through July)
  • Complete onboarding checklist items before Orientation begins
  • Attend full-day in-person Orientation (or complete online Orientation)
  • Meet with academic advisor, required before registration; a soft hold applies to non-participants
  • Register for 12–15 credit hours, including First-Year Learning Community (FLC) block aligned to your major
▸ Institutional Actions
  • Offices: New Student Orientation, SAM Center
  • Offer Orientation sessions May through July; open registration windows as early as possible
  • Send reminders with urgency messaging emphasizing early session enrollment
  • Track registered vs. not-registered; follow up with attended-but-not-enrolled students
  • Discuss upcoming semester (12–15 credits, course sequencing, prerequisites, Degree Works)
  • Enroll students in FLC block aligned to major; highlight the Bearkat Bundle on the registration dashboard
⚠ Pain Points & Barriers
  • Orientation does not consistently function as a meaningful belonging event; it should create connection before students register for classes, not only provide logistical information
  • Late-registering students are placed in online-only courses, disproportionately disadvantaging first-gen students who benefit most from in-person engagement
  • SHSU does not allow undeclared entry; students without a major are placed in Humanities with no formalized exploration pathway and no structured advising process
◈ Gaps & Disparities
  • Students who attend later Orientation sessions register with fewer open course sections, compounding the known retention risk associated with late registration
  • The 25–30% of students who register in July are at structural disadvantage entering fall; and these are disproportionately the students who most need early advising and community
✏ Team Input Needed

Students who register late (25–30% in May–July) are placed in online-only courses when in-person sections are full. What is the strategy for routing high-need students to early May or June sessions? Is there active outreach to admitted students from under-resourced high schools or first-gen households? How many students complete online vs. in-person Orientation, and do outcomes differ?

Financial Access
3 milestones
Submit FAFSA/TASFA and Receive Financial Aid Package
👤 Marisol (Low-Income, First-Gen)
Financial Access⚑ GAP✏ Team Input
▾
▸ Student Actions
  • Submit FAFSA/TASFA (Texas requires all graduating HS seniors to complete FAFSA)
  • Complete Scholarships4Kats application and any additional verification steps
  • Review financial aid award letter and accept awards
  • Complete any requirements associated with accepted aid
  • Enroll in payment plan if a remaining balance exists after aid is applied
  • Select refund preference for overpayments and student payroll if applicable
▸ Institutional Actions
  • Offices: Financial Aid & Scholarships, Student Account Services
  • Package aid quickly after admission; conduct timely review and follow up with students on missing documents
  • Send FAFSA/TASFA/Scholarships4Kats reminders and deadline nudges
  • Counsel students on aid options, cost of attendance, and direct vs. indirect costs
  • Communicate affordability clearly: net cost, not just gross award amounts
  • Assist with payment plan options; process refunds no later than 14 days after first class day or when aid applies
  • Promote targeted programs: Bearkat Promise, Local Scholar
⚠ Pain Points & Barriers
  • Freshmen submit FAFSA (Texas HS graduation requirement) but sophomores and beyond are significantly slower; the annual renewal cycle is poorly managed for returning students
  • First-gen students encounter unexpected smaller costs that financial aid does not cover; waivers exist but must be requested, and students do not know to ask
  • Former foster youth are often unaware of fee waivers and small grants available specifically to them
◈ Gaps & Disparities
  • First-gen and low-income students need financial literacy scaffolding alongside their aid award letter; not just the numbers, but what they mean and how to plan accordingly
  • Students who receive aid but face unexpected costs (supplies, commuting, technology) are not systematically connected to emergency funds or basic needs wrap-around support
✏ Team Input Needed

Financial literacy scaffolding alongside the award letter is documented as a gap, but the institutional actions describe counseling on aid options rather than a structured financial literacy intervention. Is there a standardized financial literacy module or counseling session delivered at the point of award letter issuance for first-gen and Pell-eligible students, or is this left to individual counselor discretion? For students who receive aid but face unexpected costs (supplies, commuting, technology): is there a documented referral pathway to the emergency fund, and do students know it exists at the point of receiving their award letter?
