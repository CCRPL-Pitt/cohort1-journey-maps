Stage 1 Gap Summary
Preliminary Milestones
Credential setup is manual and student-initiated, creating a systemic first barrier. Orientation is undifferentiated and does not serve working, outer-island, or non-traditional students. No documented pathway exists for students who cannot attend NTSO or TSA. NTSO attendance rates and TSA retention outcomes are not yet formally documented.
Financial Access
FAFSA incompletion and deadline misses are recurring and documented barriers. Guam-specific and compact-eligible aid is poorly explained. Financial aid and admissions timelines are misaligned. No financial literacy scaffolding accompanies the aid award. 52% of FTNC students are Pell-eligible and face the steepest consequences.
Academic Foundation
31% of FTNC students placed into developmental coursework, with limited guidance on implications for degree progress and financial aid. FY101 instruction quality is inconsistent due to adjunct delivery with no standardized training. Students receive no feedback on their four-year academic plans.
Advising & Planning
Advising assignment communication is unclear -- students frequently route academic questions to Admissions and Records, which is not equipped to answer them. Undeclared students lack a structured exploration pathway. Advising quality is inconsistent across colleges. Phillip's veteran status and prior professional experience are not systematically integrated into academic planning.
Belonging & Engagement
Welcome Week is designed for a residential campus experience that does not reflect UOG's predominantly commuter population. No structured belonging touchpoint exists for outer-island students. Stable peer-group dynamics from Guam's tight-knit communities limit organic new-relationship formation on campus.
Health, Wellness & Basic Needs
No proactive wellness or basic needs onboarding milestone exists in Stage 1. Sexual misconduct training links go to spam with no follow-up mechanism. Students face transportation and food access barriers that are unacknowledged in onboarding. Behavioral Health visits in Year 1 were difficult to meet due to limited counselor capacity; Year 2 onward improved with interns absorbing demand.
Career & Future Self
No formal career touchpoint or call-to-action exists in Stage 1. Undeclared students have the lowest six-year completion rates (33% vs. 37% overall) and the least structured early support. Phillip's military experience and transferable skills are not formalized at enrollment -- no veteran-specific career orientation exists.

Stage 2 Gap Summary
Financial Access
FAFSA renewal is not well understood as an annual requirement; SAP data is not shared with advisors. Financial holds cascade into registration blocks with no differentiated process for aid processing errors vs. non-payment. 52% of FTNC students are Pell-eligible and face the steepest consequences.
Academic Foundation
Only 37% of the Fall 2024 cohort earned 30+ credits in Year 1. Gateway course access is constrained by late registration from low credit accumulation. Early alert system is nearly unknown to faculty outside first-year courses. Stop-outs cluster in the first semester when adjunct instruction and institutional relationships are weakest.
Advising & Planning
The 31-credit early registration threshold creates a compounding disadvantage for students who earned fewer credits in Year 1 -- precisely those who most need access to open course sections. Advisors cannot see financial aid status. Degree audit and "My Progress" tools are user-unfriendly. Undeclared students receive no structured major exploration pathway.
Belonging & Engagement
Student organization participation has declined. Commuter students leave campus immediately after class. Stable peer-group dynamics from Guam's tight-knit communities limit new relationship formation. Belonging requires intentional curricular integration -- not just co-curricular programming -- as demonstrated by faculty who embed community activities into coursework.
Health, Wellness & Basic Needs
Mental health and basic needs are significantly under-documented in Year 1. No proactive wellness or food security touchpoint exists. Transportation as a barrier is unacknowledged. Students must self-identify to access any support. Behavioral Health capacity was limited in Year 1 but has improved with intern support in subsequent years.
Career & Future Self
Career engagement in Year 1 is entirely self-directed. Handshake activation rate among first-year students is not documented at the institutional level. Faculty in liberal arts and general programs rarely create career-to-major connections. No veteran-specific career pathway exists for Phillip's profile.

Stage 3 Gap Summary
Financial Access
Students who struggled to meet credit thresholds in Year 1 now face Year 2 with an already-changed aid picture and no proactive financial advising at this transition. Only 5% of the Fall 2024 cohort had accumulated 60+ credits by end of Year 2 -- the threshold needed to be on pace for 4-year graduation.
Academic Foundation
Major declaration holds create a structural trap for undeclared students. "Dare to Declare" is a once-annual spring event. Liberal arts and general program students lack the structured sequencing that nursing and education students receive. Transfer credit articulation from GCC is inconsistent and can delay graduation without warning. No structured transfer student onboarding or dedicated transfer advising role exists.
Advising & Planning
Faculty advisors are 9-month employees, creating bottlenecks during summer registration windows. Program evaluations are inconsistently reviewed, with documented instances of students enrolling in courses they did not need. Students without strong advisor relationships are most at risk of misadvising-related excess credit accumulation.
Belonging & Engagement
Students who did not build campus connections in Year 1 face a harder entry to belonging in Year 2. Student organization participation has declined institution-wide without documented study of causes or interventions. Leadership tracking data is not collected at the institutional level.
Health, Wellness & Basic Needs
Accommodation for students with caregiving or work obligations depends on individual faculty rather than institutional policy. No coordinated response exists for students experiencing food insecurity or transportation burden. These material barriers reduce capacity for academic engagement but are not acknowledged through any coordinated institutional response.
Career & Future Self
Internship coordination is decentralized and undocumented for students outside structured programs. Paid internship pipelines for low-income students are limited. Faculty in general programs rarely create career-to-major connections. Internship coordination is both centralized through Career Services and decentralized by department -- confirmed by team input.

Stage 4 Gap Summary
Financial Access
Students on extended timelines face compounding financial pressure as aid options narrow. No proactive financial transition planning exists for students who will spend more than four years in school -- a documented reality for the majority of UOG students. Students entering their 6th year are identified and provided intensive advising support, but earlier intervention does not exist.
Academic Foundation
Course availability bottlenecks in upper-division programs can add a full year to graduation timelines. Students who do not meet competitive program admission requirements in Nursing or Education have no structured transition advising. No alert, hold, or outreach mechanism is triggered when a student at 90+ credits is not on pace for graduation.
Advising & Planning
Faculty advisor unavailability during summer registration is a recurring bottleneck. Program evaluation review is inconsistent -- advisors report looking only at credit counts rather than full evaluations. Misadvising from earlier stages surfaces here as missed requirements that require additional semesters to resolve.
Belonging & Engagement
Students who never built campus connections in Years 1 and 2 face smaller peer networks in upper-division classes with no institutional outreach targeting this population. Off-campus contributions and responsibilities are invisible to the institution. There is no formal policy recognizing non-campus forms of leadership, though a cultural understanding exists that faculty are encouraged to support students with outside obligations.
Health, Wellness & Basic Needs
Veterans face specific benefit processing challenges when programs are not properly catalogued for GI Bill eligibility. Students persisting through heavy caregiving and work obligations are at elevated stop-out risk at precisely the point they are closest to completion. The early alert system remains underused even at this stage.
Career & Future Self
Post-graduation planning is largely self-directed. Graduate school exploration is not proactively surfaced for first-generation students -- there is no institutional-level faculty mentorship program, graduate school fair, or Career Services touchpoint specifically designed for this population. Paid internship pathways for low-income students who cannot afford unpaid experiences remain absent.

Stage 5 Gap Summary
Financial Access
Outstanding balances -- even from processing delays -- can block graduation clearance. Students on extended timelines face financial aid exhaustion in final years with no proactive planning support. First-generation students have a 6-point 6-year completion gap versus the overall institutional rate.
Academic Foundation
Graduation is not automatic and must be applied for -- first-generation students are least likely to know this. Capstone and internship completion are at risk for students with heavy work and caregiving obligations. Transportation barriers limit access to off-campus internship sites. Misadvising from earlier stages surfaces at the graduation audit as the last possible intervention point.
Advising & Planning
Most UOG bachelor's completions occur in Year 5 or Year 6; there is no documented graduation readiness check-in triggered at a defined credit threshold for students who are off the four-year pace -- this is an area the team has identified for exploration. Unclear faculty advisor assignments leave some students without a clear advising relationship at the finish line.
Belonging & Engagement
Students who stop out in their final semester are not systematically identified or reached out to. Graduation ceremony participation is tracked at an aggregate level -- non-attendance is attributed to personal choice without investigation of whether stop-out close to completion is occurring. Alumni Association revitalization efforts are underway but no formal graduation milestone connects students to the alumni community before they leave campus.
Health, Wellness & Basic Needs
No fast-track support pathway exists for students close to graduation who are experiencing acute financial, mental health, or logistical crises. Stopping out in the final semester is among the costliest forms of attrition -- for students in terms of foregone credential value and for the institution in terms of completion rate data.
Career & Future Self
Graduate employment outcomes by major are not systematically tracked -- identified as high-impact but not yet implemented. Career preparation in the final semester is frequently last-minute for students who did not engage with Career Services earlier. Students leaving Guam for employment or graduate school receive insufficient pre-departure advising and resource connections.
