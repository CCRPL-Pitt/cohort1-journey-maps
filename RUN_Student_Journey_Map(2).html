⚑ Stage 1 Gap Summary
Preliminary Milestones
5+ separate credentials required before Day 1 (CommunityID, NetID, DuoMobile, financial aid portal, enrollment checklist) — each a potential drop-off. Students admitted as late as July face compressed timelines for financial aid and orientation alignment. 10% who miss SOAR register late with fewer course options.
Financial Access
Financial aid and admissions timelines are fundamentally misaligned. The financial aid display system was described in session as very difficult for students to interpret. State summer financial aid being eliminated after 2026 removes the primary catch-up mechanism. 45% of Pell-eligible students carried a financial hold preventing fall registration (Spring 2026 data).
Academic Foundation
Transfer credit evaluation produces excess credits across all transfer pathways (NJCC Lampitt avg 129 credits at graduation vs. 120 required). EOF supplemental application is a barrier — CommonApp applicants frequently miss it. Developmental course awareness (One Accelerate) reaches too few eligible students before first day.
Advising & Planning
No first-year experience course outside EOF and business school — SOAR is the only universal first-year touchpoint. Parking and transportation for a primarily commuter campus named in session as a real Day 1 disadvantage not formally documented.
Belonging & Engagement
Students in special programs (EOF, HLLC, Honors, Athletics) arrive at SOAR having already built community — visible and disorienting contrast for general population students. Military-connected students auto-classified as transfers even without prior classroom experience.
Health & Basic Needs
Health requirements and immunization process tied to NetID activation. Proactive outreach for students without a primary care provider or health insurance not documented. Still open: immunization deadline clarity and consequences of non-compliance.

⚑ Stage 2 Gap Summary — Year One Enrollment
Financial Access
20% of students end up on a financial hold list preventing registration each year. Outstanding balances cascade into registration blocks and ultimately collections — advising staff cannot see financial aid status and cannot help students resolve holds. SAP checked annually in June — no mid-year early warning mechanism.
Academic Foundation
DFW rates in algebra 45–50%. Students often lack academic navigation skills (reading a syllabus, using office hours). No universal first-year experience course outside EOF and business. Major declaration policy inconsistently applied across schools. 31% of F24 students still undeclared as of March 2026.
Advising & Planning
Term-by-term program maps exist in RBS and SPAA but not consistently across SASN. Undecided vs. undeclared treated the same — advising approaches should differ. Advising quality in SASN described as under-resourced, inconsistent, and transactional. High staff turnover (6–12 month HR hiring delay) compounds service gaps.
Belonging & Engagement
Commuter students face the greatest belonging gap. Belonging survey in progress — early data shows high classroom belonging but low peer connectedness. First-year students not eligible for leadership roles. Student organizations are the primary driver of belonging but face structural friction (2-month space booking lead times, flat budgets).
Health & Basic Needs
Madison Holleran compliance creates a formal touchpoint but referral follow-through varies widely. Most students reach wellness supports through classroom referrals, not proactive outreach. Faculty referral quality inconsistent across departments. Mental health withdrawals: 225 cases in past 5 years (3rd leading cause).
Career & Future Self
CRE is optional — must work harder to reach students. Significant gaps between majors (especially A&S) and students' understanding of career pathways. Faculty in A&S rarely discuss industry with students; RBS culture is starkly different — creating an inequitable experience by school.

⚑ Stage 3 Gap Summary — Year Two Enrollment
Financial Access
Same cascading billing, hold, and registration block system continues. 20% of students end on a financial hold list. Among the 2,600+ students who withdrew over the past five years, financial difficulties were the leading reason (341 cases), followed by mental health (225) and family responsibilities (218). Stage 3 is where all three pressures converge most acutely. Note: The 2,600+ figure captures only students who formally withdrew mid-semester — students who stopped attending without formally withdrawing are not captured (John Gunkel and Chengbo Yin, June 11, 2026).
Academic Foundation
Major declaration deadline approaching (60 credits / SASN). 31% of F24 students still undeclared as of March 2026. Major declaration is not an online process. Students who have not passed foundational courses may stop out at this stage.
Advising & Planning
Key services (advising, one-stop, enrollment management) not available during hours when many commuter students are on campus. One faculty member described staying until 11 PM to provide advising otherwise inaccessible to evening students. Union contracts limit shift flexibility. High staff turnover with 6–12 month HR hiring delays produces prolonged service gaps (Vincent Tepedino, John Gunkel, June 11, 2026).
Belonging & Engagement
Year 2 is when students first become eligible for e-board and leadership roles. Students who did not build community in Year 1 face a harder on-ramp. Student organizations are the primary driver of belonging but face structural friction: space bookings require up to 2 months advance planning, spaces are charged to orgs, and budgets have not grown alongside enrollment (~150+ orgs on a budget similar to 5 years ago). Programming overwhelmingly concentrated during daytime free periods, creating access barriers for commuter and nontraditional students (Luke Greeley, Erica, John Gunkel, June 11, 2026).
Career & Future Self
Braven Career Accelerator (3-credit, Year 2) is the primary career intervention but students are regularly dropped due to course conflicts. Not covered by GI Bill — excluding veteran students. The career services perception gap is at least partly structural: business school has its own dedicated career office; A&S departments have historically had little intentional engagement with central CRE (John Gunkel, Erica, Vincent Tepedino, June 11, 2026).
Systems-Level
Legacy student information system (built in the 1980s) syncs across constituent systems only once per day at ~midnight — staff always working from yesterday's data. Real-time intervention on holds, registration blocks, or financial aid changes is not possible. Data heavily siloed across admissions, registrar, financial aid, and advising systems with no common querying interface (John Gunkel, Vincent Tepedino, Chengbo Yin, June 11, 2026).

⚑ Stage 4 Gap Summary — Year Three Enrollment
Financial Access
Increased need for aid as scholarships are exhausted. Transfer students approaching aid eligibility limits. Financial aid process is the same each year but students who have not resolved prior holds are increasingly at risk of stopping out.
Academic Foundation
Upper-division courses require prerequisites students may not have passed. Schedule compression means two required courses may be offered simultaneously. Anecdotally ~25% of students face graduation delays due to course availability. Transfer students face significantly higher failure rates in senior-level STEM courses (chemistry: 38.6% vs. 23.9%; biology: 11.8% vs. 8.4%).
Advising & Planning
Advising transitions from general to program-specific — quality varies widely by department. Transfer students arriving in Year 3 have limited time to build campus-based experience before graduation. Graduate school application process begins for some students.
Belonging & Engagement
Budget constraints have not kept pace with org growth (~150+ orgs running on a budget similar to 5 years ago). Leadership succession is an active concern. Transfer students entering at Year 3 face a compounded belonging challenge — arriving without shared social history and entering an org ecosystem mid-cycle where leadership structures are already established (John Gunkel, Erica, June 11, 2026).
Career & Future Self
Career readiness gap between RBS and A&S students deepens. Students who have not been systematically connected to CRE or supported in building experiential learning by Year 3 arrive with significantly less career preparation. High-impact experiential learning support is uneven across schools. A&S students arrive at Year 3 with significantly less career preparation than RBS peers — a structural, not individual, gap.
Systems-Level
Legacy data systems described in Stage 3 Gap Summary continue to affect support in Years 3–4. The inability to pull real-time, cross-system student data makes proactive intervention for at-risk students in upper-division coursework, financial aid transitions, and graduation eligibility equally difficult at this stage.

⚑ Stage 5 Gap Summary — Year Four to Graduation
Financial Access
Accumulated balances, holds, and registration blocks reach their most painful point. Transfer students at risk of running out of aid eligibility. Online loan exit counseling does not ensure genuine understanding of repayment obligations — "nobody ever reads the form" (in-person session). Students funding final year entirely out of pocket due to prior SAP failures.
Academic Foundation
Lack of consistent academic plans throughout the journey means the graduation audit surfaces surprises. 49.5% of senior students received no individual advising in Fall 2025 — for nearly half of students in their final year, the graduation audit is the primary safety net. Students who earned degrees recently completed an average of 130 credits vs. 120 required — excess credit has direct financial consequences. Course availability issues are most painful here.
Belonging & Engagement
Commencement and transition. Identity-affirming celebrations important for underrepresented populations. Alumni office struggles to maintain contact after @rutgers.edu email expires. Org succession planning is active concern — leadership knowledge often leaves with graduating classes.
Health & Basic Needs
Students who relied on campus housing face housing insecurity post-graduation. Health services available through June 30 only. Wellness continuity planning is difficult for students not engaged throughout their journey. 55% of undergrad students indicated they wanted information about the food pantry — demand for basic needs support substantially higher than current utilization.
Career & Future Self
Best case: students who followed the journey have a job offer before they graduate. For those who have not, it becomes a serious problem. Alumni engagement and contact information management is a pain point for ongoing post-graduation support. A&S students without a clear career pipeline who were never systematically connected to CRE face the steepest climb.
